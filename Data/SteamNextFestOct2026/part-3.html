Section 01

The Follower Base

You mostly are who you were before the fest.

Zukowski grouped every October demo by peak concurrent players, then looked at their Steam follower count the day before the festival. Steam followers are a public stand-in for wishlists — which only the developer can see — and the gradient is stark: the top of the field arrived with thousands of followers already banked.

Section 02

What Actually Predicts Peak Players

Base and momentum, about equally — % growth doesn't.

Zukowski tested which number best tracks a demo's early peak players (peak CCU across 12–21 October — press preview plus the first couple of days). He used a Spearman rank correlation (ρ): 1.0 means two rankings match perfectly, 0 means no relationship, so 0.55 is meaningful but far from decisive. His conclusion: a pre-fest follower base and pre-fest momentum are about the same, with a slight edge to having followers already. The best situation is both.

HighThe percentage trap: the bottom group showed the highest percentage growth of any tier (12.5%) — but from a median of 15 followers, that's one extra person. A final-week spike looks good on a chart and does almost nothing for your peak. Track raw gains, not percentages.
HighThis is early data, by design: Zukowski measured peak demo CCU from 12–21 October only, and explicitly flags that his follow-up on total follower gain comes after the festival closes. The correlation says base and momentum predict early interest about equally — not that base alone "decides" the outcome. Hold the conclusion a couple of degrees softer than the numbers, until the post-fest analysis lands.

Read through the lens of Parts I and II, the implication stays coherent: the festival's engagement-ranked Day 3+ phase rewards the demos already converting — which tend to be the ones that entered with either a large base or genuine momentum, ideally both. Scale has made the starting funnel the whole game.

Section 03

AI Disclosure — Concentrated at the Bottom

Disclosure doesn't doom a game; it clusters where attention is thinnest.

Steam requires developers to declare generative AI use on the store page (Steamworks content survey). Zukowski counted 2,502 games (~35%) with a disclosure in October 2026 — self-reported, so the true rate could differ — but the split by performance tier is unambiguous.

LikelyCorrelation is not causation. There's no evidence that disclosing AI causes weak results — plenty of low-ranked games disclosed nothing, and exactly two AI-disclosed titles made the top 25: BORE (built "with help from an AI assistant under human direction"; no live AI generation) and Path of the Yaoguai ("部分游戏图片由AI生成并修改" — some game images AI-generated and modified). What the data shows is that most disclosed AI work sits in the crowded bottom of the festival. If your game uses AI art, it's competing inside that crowd — which is precisely why a pre-built audience matters more, not less.

Section 05

The February Playbook

Start earlier, count the right number.

The next edition is expected in February. October's data points to a concrete sequence of actions — none of them last-minute — distilled into what to start and what to stop.

Start now, not in January

  • Count followers every week. Base and momentum both predict early peak about equally; both need time to build.
  • Schedule wishlist campaigns, festival slots and press for Nov–Dec. That does more for a February demo than a late-January burst of posts.
  • Don't mistake demo age for progress. The top 25 had their demos up for less time (~19 days) than everyone else (~37). A demo only helps if you're driving people to it.

Match your genre's math

  • If few demos in your genre reach 10 players (platformers, walking sims), budget more pre-festival marketing — or delay to a later fest to build a bigger base.
  • If you're in co-op, party, survival-craft or desktop/idle, lead with that hook in the first ten seconds of the trailer.

Measure the right thing

  • Track raw follower gains, not percentages. A 50% jump from 80 to 120 won't move your peak.
  • Set a "day-before-fest" baseline and judge the festival against it, not against the leaderboard.

Use the tooling

  • Zukowski's free Next Fest tracker compares your traffic and wishlist numbers against the Feb and Jun 2026 editions.
  • Be honest on AI disclosure. Steam requires it, and top-ranked games that disclosed still performed.
  • Watch for the post-fest follow-up on total follower gains once this edition closes.
DirectionalThe through-line of the whole case study: three years of growth, an eightfold field, and a two-tier result. Early-festival data says pre-fest follower base and pre-fest momentum predict early peak players about equally — the worst position is a game that is neither known ahead of time nor catching on. Whatever you bring, bring it before the festival: arrive with an audience, or ride real momentum in. Just don't show up empty and hope for a last-week spike.

What Would Change This

Four dated checks on this case study.

Each condition below has a date and a public source. If it goes the other way, the claim it tests should be revised in the open — the same way Section 04 grades June.

Resolves by 30 November 2026 Provisional
Zukowski publishes his post-fest analysis of total follower gains
Part III's central finding — base and momentum predict early peaks about equally — rests on early-fest peak CCU. If full-fest follower gains track the pre-fest base far more closely than momentum (or the reverse), the "about equally" reading gets revised.

The final count of 2026 Steam releases is known
At ~29,300 releases, entries equal ~51.7% and Part I's "threshold" holds. At ~40,000, the ratio falls to about 38% — no change from 2025 — and the threshold claim goes.
Resolves at Next Fest, 22 February – 1 March 2027
Participation and AI disclosure in the February edition
A February field below 7,240 would weaken the reading that AI-assisted entrants are driving growth. Top-25 AI disclosure rising above ~15% (from 8%) would weaken "AI work pools at the bottom".
Resolves at Next Fest, February 2027
Idle and desktop games in the top 10 by peak players
Three idle/desktop games sat in October's top 10 by peak. If that repeats, background-running games have a structural edge in concurrent-player rankings. If they disappear — or Valve changes how it measures "most played" — the idle vein was a one-edition fluke.
