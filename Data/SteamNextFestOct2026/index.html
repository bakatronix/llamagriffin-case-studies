Steam Next Fest October 2026The Largest — and the Most Split — Edition Yet.

The October 2026 edition ran 19–26 October with 7,240 games taking part — the largest Next Fest in the program's history, up 65% on June's 4,382 and roughly eight times the 887 of June 2023.

That scale crossed a threshold: across 2026's three editions, Next Fest entries equalled more than half of the year's projected Steam releases. And it changed what the event does — it stopped creating breakouts and started amplifying the audience a game already had, measured the day before it even began.

Our June case study forecast this edition at about 5,500 entries; it came in at 7,240. Part III scores that forecast and June\'s other calls. This case study tells that story in three parts — the record scale, who actually got seen, and what predicted early success — and closes with why this edition was a genuinely different animal.

Why this edition was different
I
ProvisionalA different species of scale
Not just a record — a threshold. The year's entries equal ~51.7% of projected 2026 Steam releases, up from 38.2% in 2025 and 33.6% in 2024. It's a ratio, not a headcount — a game in two editions counts twice, and the denominator is projected — but the direction is clear.
II
HighThe two-tier field went from trend to headline
Past editions looked for a single breakout. This one made the split unavoidable: ~100 games held real audiences, and ~6,900 arrived with a median of 15 followers — a gap already in place before the first slot was served.
III
HighAI disclosure became a structural layer
35% of games disclosed generative AI, concentrated at the bottom (8% of the top 25 vs 35% of everyone else). Up from about a quarter in June — "slop" is now a measurable layer of the field, not a hand-wave.
IV
DirectionalAn idle/desktop vein cracked the top
Character House, Find The Needle and Melvor Idle 2 sat near the top of the live leaderboard all week — partly because idle games keep counting while left running — while early-fest leaders like Worst Class Ever and ArcheAge faded to a tenth or less of their peaks. A new leaderboard shape, not the old spike-and-fade.
