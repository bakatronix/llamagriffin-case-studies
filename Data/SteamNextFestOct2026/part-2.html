Section 01

The Shape of a Winner

A mechanical hook legible in ten seconds.

The top of the chart rewards demos whose pitch survives contact with a crowded store page. Across the most-played list, a consistent formula repeats: a differentiating mechanic visible in a single screenshot or ten-second trailer, frequently layered on a co-op loop.

DirectionalThe ten-second test: A teacher hunting hidden students, a raft powered by flatulence, a haystack made of six million strands you click through. Each top demo passes the "can I explain it in ten seconds" test before any genre tag or description is read. Games that leaned on their genre as the selling point did not break out.

The surprise of this edition was a second vein quietly riding near the top: idle and desktop-companion games. Character House (a chibi desk companion), Find The Needle (a haystack clicker), and Melvor Idle 2 all cracked the top of the live leaderboard — a category whose concurrent counts stay high partly because the games keep counting while left running in the background.

Section 03

The Friendslop Era

Multiplayer is the discovery engine.

The single most consistent signal in the October 2026 leaderboard is the presence of a second player. "Friendslop" is the community's nickname — half affectionate, half derisive — for cheap, chaotic co-op games built to be played and streamed with friends. At least 14 of the 20 most-played demos support co-op or party play, and the trend compounds on itself — recommendation and streaming favour games that look fun to watch in groups.

DirectionalFriendslop isn't a fad, it's feedback: the term — derisive among some developers — now describes the market's working discovery loop. Co-op games get watched, clipped, and shared; that spillover is exactly what the engagement-ranked half of the festival algorithm rewards from Day 3 onward. The October edition is the clearest confirmation yet.

Section 05

Conversion — Who Reaches 10 Players

The hit-rate by genre.

A second, independent lens comes from Zukowski's scrape: the share of demos in each genre that reached 10 or more concurrent players. It answers a different question from the leaderboard: not which games peaked highest, but which genres give a typical demo the best odds — and which structurally struggle.

Platformer reality check: a platformer can still succeed at Next Fest — but because only 1.5–1.9% of the category reaches even 10 concurrent players, it must arrive with a stronger-than-average audience already in place. The festival crowd will not surface it. That's the earliest actionable reading of October's data, and it carries straight into Part III.
