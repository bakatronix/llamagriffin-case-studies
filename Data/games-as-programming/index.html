Games as Television ProgrammingWhen Games Stopped Being Products.

Netflix closed five game studios in five years and kept every category that behaves like an episode. Then it premiered the GTA VI reveal through its documentaries department, and a trailer for an unreleased game became the most-watched thing on the service that week in 87 of 93 countries.

This report tests one hypothesis across three companies: games are being reclassified from products you seek out into programming that arrives, and the filter being applied is not genre or quality but whether a thing behaves like an episode. Amazon and Microsoft moved on the same afternoon in July, independently, toward adjacent versions of the same shift. Six weeks later Microsoft put a meter on it.

Part I tests it against what Netflix shut and kept. Part II tests it against Amazon, Microsoft, and market data that disagrees with itself by nearly six times. Part III tests it against the compute the whole model runs on — and against this report's own failed drafts.

The filter was never about genre or quality. Every studio Netflix exited built games as products — things a person has to go and get, install, learn, and return to. Every category it kept makes something that arrives inside an app the household was already in. Separately, Netflix has decided it does not need to own the studios producing that programming. The industry heard a retreat from games because that was the sentence it was expecting.
Three claims were removed in review. They are recorded in the text rather than in a footnote, and Part III carries all three.
  1. Substitution. A chain in which players priced out of a $799.99 console move to streaming instead — killed by Microsoft's own disclosure that the cap affects 4% of subscribers.
  2. Compute scarcity. The compute-scarcity rebuttal to the acquisition thesis — killed because compute is rentable and intellectual property is not, which makes scarcity an argument for acquisition rather than against it.
  3. The GTA 6 test. A console-exclusive megahit fits this thesis and its exact opposite equally well, so it discriminates between nothing.

The instrument that would settle most of this argument is the one nobody publishes: cloud session length, and Netflix game hours per subscriber. Peters was asked for the second in July and declined. Until those numbers exist, every thesis in this space is inference dressed as measurement, including this one.
