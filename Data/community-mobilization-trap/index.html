The Mobilization Trap

Community acquires. It does not hold. The gap is the same in games and in governments.

THE ARGUMENT

Four live-service games acquired forty-eight million players between them and shut down.

Two governments fell inside a week and lost their countries within six months. The same gap does both, and it is not the gap the industry talks about.

The finding

Mass coordination is now cheap and fast in both domains. The multi-year organizational labour that converts coordination into anything durable did not get cheaper at all. Whoever has already done that labour captures the moment, regardless of who generated it.

METHOD

The corrections are the report

This report was commissioned to test a thesis, and a team was tasked with destroying it. Six of eight component claims fell. The replacement claim then failed its own second review for selecting on the outcome, and a third pass falsified the replacement's central mechanism.

All three corrections sit in the text rather than in a footnote, because the corrections are more useful than the original argument was. What is left is narrower, older and more actionable than what we started with.

The instrument that would settle most of this argument is the one nobody publishes: what a launch cost to market, next to what it earned. No publisher in this report disclosed one. Until that exists, every thesis in this space is inference dressed as measurement, including this one.
