The Mobilization Trap · Part I of Three

The Acquisition Trap

They all acquired. None of them held.

ROUGHLY 12 MINUTES · RECOGNIZING PATTERNS · LLAMA & GRIFFIN

SECTION 01

Forty-eight million players, four shutdowns

Start with the cases that cannot be explained by failed marketing, because the marketing worked.

TitleAcquiredOutcome
XDefiant11M players, 1M in a 2023 closed betaShut within twelve months, 277 jobs across three studios
MultiVersus20M players, 153,433 Steam peak686 concurrents at the end, $100M-plus impairment, shut May 2025
Knockout City12M lifetime players, critically well receivedShut June 2023; the studio called live games immensely challenging
Spellbreak5M players in three weeksAveraged 166 concurrents before closure

These four figures use four definitions and are not additively comparable. They are presented together because the scale of the gap survives any reasonable reconciliation, not because the sum is a unique-user count.

Not one of these is a discovery failure, an awareness failure or a community failure. Every one of them is a retention failure, and retention was never the thing being funded.

Ubisoft's language on closing XDefiant is the tell. Games-as-a-service remains a pillar of our strategy, announced in the same breath as the shutdown.

The consulting tell

When a company loses 11 million players and reaffirms the model, it has diagnosed the failure as execution. The pattern across four studios says otherwise. Acquisition is a solved problem that the industry keeps re-solving, because it is the part it knows how to buy.

Star Citizen is often filed here. It should not be. Over $1B raised from 6.5M people and still in early access after nine years is a governance and scope failure, not a retention failure. It is retaining its cohort fine. That is the problem.

SECTION 03

The toolset hypothesis, and why it fails

The natural next move is to say the survivors shipped something players could author into: mods, deep systems, build space, player-run economies. It is a clean story and the launch dates destroy it.

Among the long-tail games, the participation layer usually arrived years after the audience did

Two of eight shipped it at launch. Three never shipped it at all.

Among the failures, several shipped it early and comprehensively

Worlds Adrift is the cleanest refutation available. The Workshop-backed Island Creator shipped 28 April 2016, more than a year before the game, and every island in the world was player-made. It never exceeded 2,057 concurrents and shut in July 2019 as no longer commercially viable.

Hytale is the live case and the most instructive. Riot cancelled it and began closing Hypixel Studios in June 2025; co-founder Simon Collins-Laflamme bought it back and shipped a four-year-old legacy-engine build. Server-side modding, community servers, live editors, no-code trigger volumes and monetisable mods all shipped at early access launch on 13 January 2026. Modding uptake was real: 20 million CurseForge mod downloads and more than 2,000 creators within weeks.

A measurement caution on Hytale

Hytale is not on Steam, so no concurrent series exists. The launch figure of roughly 2.8 million concurrent is a co-founder statement reported by one outlet, not an independently verified number. The only current figure available is a third-party tracker reporting 9 players online across 150 live community servers, which counts listed multiplayer servers only and excludes singleplayer entirely.

Those two numbers measure different populations and should not be presented as a single decline curve. What can be said with confidence is narrower: the authoring layer shipped in full at launch, uptake was genuine, and concurrency fell from its January highs regardless.

Crowfall inverts the thesis completely. The player-facing campaign system was the stated cause of death: the sheer amount of development effort required to build new campaigns and keep the game running daily. Offline November 2022, about sixteen months after launch.

And the list runs on. Landmark, where the game itself was the creation toolkit, dead in eight months. Dual Universe, with an NPC-free player economy and Lua scripting at launch, peak 792. Atlas, with the full ARK-style DevKit, Workshop and private-server stack, down 99.5% from a 58,939 peak. WildStar, with a currency economy, deep housing and player-built War Plots, closed November 2018. Budget does not rescue it either: Battlefield 2042 shipped Portal on day one and fell from 105,400 concurrents to about 19,000 in six weeks.

The finding that survives

Participation infrastructure is neither necessary nor sufficient for retention. Three of the longest-lived games in the sample never shipped one. Eleven of the twelve counter-cases did. Any deck claiming otherwise is selecting on the outcome.

SECTION 04

What actually separates them

Plot the two things that are supposed to be related. On one axis, how long after launch the all-time concurrent peak arrived. On the other, how much of that peak the game still holds today.

FIGURE 1 · TWENTY-ONE TITLES, STEAM CONCURRENT SERIES READ 23 AUGUST 2026. OLD SCHOOL RUNESCAPE AND HYTALE ARE EXCLUDED BECAUSE THEIR PEAK AND CURRENT FIGURES COME FROM DIFFERENT CLIENT POPULATIONS.

Every quadrant is occupied, and that is the result.

Late peak, high retention. RimWorld at 32% of peak, Warframe at 26%, Factorio at 19%, Path of Exile at 12% read at a league trough. This is the group the toolset story was built to explain, and it does contain the games with the deepest player-authored ecosystems.

Late peak, dead anyway. Battlefield 2042 reached its all-time Steam peak in October 2023, nearly two years after launch, and holds 1.1% of it. Deep Rock Galactic peaked six years in and is at 3,393 concurrent players. A late peak is not a guarantee of anything.

Launch peak, high retention. Helldivers 2 peaked sixteen days after release and currently holds 87,862 concurrent, the largest number in this entire sample. Overwatch 2 sits at 25% of its Steam peak. Peaking at launch is not a death sentence.

Launch peak, nothing retained. Twelve games, every one below 0.5% of peak, and eleven of the twelve shipped a genuine authoring layer.

Only that last corner is clean, and it is the corner that carries the argument. Peak shape describes a regime. It does not predict retention, and the version of this claim in the first draft of this report, that every game with a late peak is still large, does not survive its own data.

What does track survival

The one variable in this sample that appears in the long-tail group and never in the failures is sustained content cadence over years. Path of Exile's 13-week league cycle, forty releases in ten years. Old School RuneScape's poll-and-update loop. Warframe's update rhythm across a decade.

This is unglamorous, expensive, continuous labour. Player authoring lowers the cost of that cadence. It does not substitute for it. Crowfall is the proof: it had the authoring layer and died of the cadence bill.

The pattern to price

Retention is not a feature you ship. It is a cost you commit to for years, and almost nobody underwrites it at greenlight because the greenlight case is built on acquisition maths.

SECTION 05

IP is the third path

Push marketing has not stopped working. It works reliably when it has something to push.

Hogwarts Legacy launched into an organized boycott, which is to say the loudest community activity around it was hostile, and took $850M with more than 12M units in two weeks on a YouTube-led paid campaign. Circana named it the best-selling US game of 2023 at more than 22M copies, ending a fourteen-year streak held by Call of Duty and Rockstar, on lukewarm initial reviews.

Monster Hunter Wilds took more than 10M in its first month, a Capcom record. Diablo IV did $666M of sell-through in five days. Pokemon Scarlet and Violet did 10M in three days on a three-out-of-five review citing performance problems. Assassin's Creed Shadows passed 5M players through a sustained negativity campaign. On mobile, Monopoly Go generated $2B on user-acquisition spend reported between $500M and $1.5bn.

Valve's own conversion data belongs here rather than in the wishlist teardown. NBA 2K26 and EA Sports FC 25 exceeded 400% wishlist conversion, which Valve reads as most buyers never having wishlisted them. That is not an argument against the wishlist instrument. It is an argument for IP substitution: recognition arrives through a channel the instrument cannot see.

Close

There are three ways to solve a cold start. Buy recognition, build participation, or borrow a community you did not make. All three get you a launch. Only sustained cadence gets you a year three, and IP buys the runway to afford one.
